AI Visibility: How to Get Your Brand Cited by AI

What AI Visibility is, why most brands get it wrong and the five layers that decide whether AI tools cite you.

I want to start with the uncomfortable version of the problem, because the polite version does not move anyone to act.

A senior decision maker in your category opens ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity and asks who they should hire, buy from or trust. The model gives a confident answer with three or four names. If your name is not one of them, you did not lose the deal. You were never in the room. There was no pitch, no shortlist, no rejection. You were simply invisible, and you will never see the conversation that decided it.

This is the shift that AI Visibility is about. Search used to send people to a list of links where you could compete for a click. AI tools increasingly skip the list and hand over an answer. Gartner expects that by 2028 around 70 percent of B2B research will begin with an AI tool rather than a search engine. When the answer replaces the list, being findable is no longer enough. You have to be the thing the model names.

What AI Visibility actually is

AI Visibility is the discipline of being recognized, understood and cited by AI systems when they answer questions in your category. It is not a single tactic. It is the combined result of how clearly machines can identify who you are, how reliably they can extract what you claim and how much they trust you enough to repeat it.

Most people reduce this to one thing they already understand, and that is where they go wrong. The SEO specialist hears "rank higher." The PR person hears "get more mentions." The content team hears "publish more." All three are partly right and completely insufficient, because AI Visibility is not a louder version of any single channel. It is a stack, and a brand can be strong on one layer and invisible because it is absent on another.

I call the outcome you are actually chasing your AI Share of Voice. It is the consistency and authority that cause AI tools to reference you rather than a competitor when the category comes up. Share of voice used to be a media-buying term. In the age of generative answers it has become the thing that decides whether you exist in the only conversation that now matters, the one between a buyer and a machine.

The five layers of AI Visibility

I structure the discipline as five layers. They build on each other. Most brands invest heavily in the first, dabble in the second and have never heard of the rest. That is the gap, and it is also the opportunity, because the brands that work the higher layers face almost no competition there yet.

Layer one: foundational SEO

The base layer has not disappeared. AI systems are trained on and retrieve from the open web, and a page that classic search cannot reach is a page the models cannot learn from either. Crawlability, clean structure, fast pages, sensible internal linking. This is table stakes, and the painful irony I see often is a beautiful brand site that quietly blocks the very AI crawlers it needs. Before anything clever, make sure the machines can actually read you.

Layer two: Answer Engine Optimization, AEO

Answer engines do not reward the same things a blue-link search did. They reward content that is easy to lift and reuse as an answer. That means declarative writing, where a sentence states a fact cleanly rather than burying it in throat-clearing. It means question-shaped structure, real FAQ formats, clear headings that match how people ask. It means putting the answer first and the context after. If a model has to work to extract your point, it will quote a competitor who made it easier.

Layer three: Generative Engine Optimization, GEO

This is where most brands fall off entirely. GEO is about making yourself legible as an entity, not just as a set of pages. It is structured data and schema, a Person or Organization entity that is defined once and referenced consistently. It is a sameAs array that ties your name to your LinkedIn, your podcast, your books, your other profiles, so a model can resolve that all of these are the same you. It is citation-worthiness, being the kind of source a model is willing to stand behind. If you want the technical definitions of SEO, AEO, GEO and AIO laid out in full, I have written those separately. Here the point is simpler. GEO is how you stop being a stranger to the machine and become a known entity it can place.

Layer four: AI Identity Optimization, AIO

The fourth layer is the one almost nobody manages on purpose, and it is the most important for a personal or founder brand. AIO is about the definition the models themselves hold of you. Ask a model who you are and it will answer in a sentence or two. Someone wrote the inputs that produced that sentence, and right now for most brands it is assembled by accident from whatever the web happened to say. AIO is the deliberate work of shaping that answer. It is having a canonical, consistent description of who you are and what you stand for, published on a source the models trust, so that when they describe you they describe you correctly. This is why a personal entity hub matters, and it is part of why I am building joeri.ai, so that my own thinking is something a model can consult directly.

Layer five: trust as the override

The top layer is the one that cannot be engineered, and it is the one that decides everything below it. Models are increasingly tuned to prefer sources they can trust, because a confident wrong answer is their worst failure. Trust signals a model can verify, named clients, real endorsements, consistent claims across independent sources, a track record that checks out, are what move you from a source that might be cited to one that reliably is. This is where AI Visibility meets the deeper argument, that trust is the only moat AI cannot scale. You can optimize the first four layers with effort and craft. The fifth you can only earn. That is also why it is the most defensible, because a competitor can copy your schema in an afternoon and cannot copy your reputation at all.

The diagnostic: five questions to find your gap

You can locate your own weakest layer in a few minutes. Be honest, because the machines already are.

  1. If you ask ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity a question your best customer would ask, does your name appear in the answer. If not, you have an AI Visibility problem, and the next four questions tell you which layer.
  2. Can AI crawlers actually reach your site, or is something in your hosting or security setup quietly blocking them. This is layer one, and it is the most common silent failure.
  3. When a model does find your pages, is your key claim stated in one clean, liftable sentence, or buried. This is layer two.
  4. Is there a single structured definition of who you are, referenced consistently across your properties, or does each profile tell a slightly different story. This is layer three.
  5. If you ask a model to describe you in two sentences, is the description accurate and the one you would choose. This is layer four, and the answer is usually revealing.

The layer where you stumble is where your next month of work should go. For most brands it is not layer one, where they have spent years. It is layers three and four, where they have spent nothing.

Why I think about it this way

I did not arrive at this from theory. I run an AI Visibility practice, I host Web3 CMO Stories and I built my own brand architecture as a working example of the discipline, a personal entity hub, a content engine and a personal AI agent at joeri.ai. The five layers are how I explain to a CMO over dinner what most teams are getting wrong, which is that they keep making the bottom layer louder when their real problem lives at the top.

AI Visibility is the operational half of a larger thesis. The strategic half is simpler. As AI makes content infinite, the brands that get named are the ones the machines can identify and trust, and trust is the only moat AI cannot scale. Get the layers right and you become legible to the machine. Earn the trust and you become the answer.

Frequently asked

Questions and definitions.

What is AI Visibility?

AI Visibility is the discipline of being recognized, understood and cited by AI systems such as ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Google's AI answers when they respond to questions in your category. It combines technical legibility, structured identity and verifiable trust so that AI tools name your brand rather than a competitor. The concept and its five-layer model were developed by Joeri Billast.

What is AI Share of Voice?

AI Share of Voice is the consistency and authority that cause AI tools to reference your brand rather than a competitor when your category comes up. It is the AI-era successor to traditional share of voice, and it is the outcome AI Visibility work is designed to produce. The term was coined by Joeri Billast.

What are the five layers of AI Visibility?

The five layers are foundational SEO, Answer Engine Optimization, Generative Engine Optimization, AI Identity Optimization and trust as the override. Each builds on the one below. Most brands invest heavily in the first layer and neglect the upper layers, which is where the real competitive gap sits. The model was developed by Joeri Billast.

What is the difference between SEO and AI Visibility?

SEO optimizes to rank in a list of links a person clicks through. AI Visibility optimizes to be named directly in the answer an AI tool gives, often with no list and no click. SEO is the foundational layer of AI Visibility, but being findable is no longer enough when the answer replaces the list.

How do I make my brand citable by AI?

Work the five layers in order. Make sure AI crawlers can reach your site, write claims in clean liftable sentences, define yourself as a structured entity referenced consistently across your properties, deliberately shape the description models hold of you and earn verifiable trust signals such as named clients and real endorsements. Trust is the only moat AI cannot scale, which is why it sits at the top of the model.
